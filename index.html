Biology of History and Adaptation

How biological systems remember, adapt, and survive.

I study the biology of cellular history and adaptation. How do biological systems remember previous events and their consequences? And how do they use this memory for adaptation and survival?

01

How is biological memory formed?

Biological systems experience signals that may be mechanical, chemical, physical, infectious, or a combination of these signals. How do these signals induce a new biological state that persists even after the signal has been removed? In general, we call this memory. It may be immune, mechanical, metabolic, or exist across different levels, from the cell to the organ. How are these memories stored?

02

How does memory influence adaptation?

How do these memories influence the present and future of the system? Does memory always have an advantageous effect, or can it sometimes have deleterious consequences? We also call this maladaptation. What factors affect this memory-driven new biological state?

03

Can maladaptive memory be reset?

If these memories have deleterious consequences, can they be reset by resetting the altered memory?

Personal principles
